Chapter 01

Our story

Three sentences. Everything else on this page grows from them.

Auntie Gaik Lean began cooking for her family at twelve.

1She is a fourth-generation Nyonya and taught herself. Every recipe in the kitchen is an heirloom from her family.

2In 2013 her eldest son, Adrian Tan, opened the eatery on Bishop Street, named it after her and asked her to come home to run it.

3The first MICHELIN Guide for Malaysia gave it One MICHELIN Star, the first for a Nyonya restaurant in Malaysia. It has kept it every year since.

  1. 12Age

    Begins cooking for her family. A fourth-generation Nyonya, she teaches herself.

  2. 18Age

    Marries, and sells homemade kuih by the roadside.

  3. 21Age

    Cooks in a factory canteen at Motorola, then other factory kitchens.

  4. 40sHer

    Trains chefs in Kuala Lumpur.

  5. 2013Year

    Her eldest son, Adrian Tan, buys the place on Bishop Street, names it after her and asks her to come home to run it.

  6. 2022December

    The first MICHELIN Guide for Malaysia gives the eatery One MICHELIN Star: the first Nyonya restaurant in Malaysia to earn one. Auntie is 69.

  7. 2026Today

    Kept every year since. Four years running, 2023 to 2026 guides.

One MICHELIN Star

Four years running.

The 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 MICHELIN Guides for Malaysia. Write MICHELIN in capitals. Never draw the MICHELIN logo, Bibendum or the star flower.

Values & voice

What the kitchen keeps to, and how it sounds when we write it down.

  1. 01Made by handEverything in the kitchen is prepared by hand.
  2. 02From scratch, on siteMade from scratch in our own kitchen on Bishop Street.
  3. 03Family recipesHeirloom recipes from a fourth-generation Nyonya cook.
  4. 04One shared tableDishes go to the middle and everyone shares. Large tables seat ten or more.
  5. 05A pork-free kitchenWe say pork-free. We never say halal.

The tone

  • Warm.Like Auntie talking across the table.
  • Plain.Short sentences. No exclamation marks.
  • A little proud.State the fact. Let it speak.
  • UK English.Flavour, colour, Wed–Sun, 12pm.

Chapter 04

Taglines

Six lines, each with one job. Pick the one that fits the piece.

  • One line per piece. Never stack two taglines.
  • Never change the words. The full stop stays.
  • Italic marks one word. The word you want heard.
  • Never over food. Set it on the ground beside the photo.

Chapter 05

Logo system

One logo: the porcelain oval over Auntie’s name in script. Always the real file, never retyped.

Which logo on which ground

Use Ink logo. The house setting on light grounds.

Clear space

Measure x: the height of the porcelain oval. Keep x clear on every side. No text, no edge, no photo detail enters the box.

x
Height of the oval, about 40% of the logo height
Clear space
1x on all four sides
Proportion
3.1 : 1, locked. Scale from a corner.

Minimum sizes

Never do this

  • Stretched or squashed

  • Recoloured

  • Rotated or tilted

  • Drop shadows or glows

  • Auntie Gaik Lean’s

    Retyped in a font

  • Over food or a busy photo

  • Ink on a dark ground

  • Cropped or split

Chapter 10

Motion

Unhurried, like a good kitchen. Things arrive, settle and stay still. Nothing bounces.

Tokens

Arrivecubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1)Reveals, panels, photos
Doorscubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)Doors, tiers, loops
Touch160 msHover, press, focus
Move400 msMenus, drawers, tabs
Reveal900 msRise 14 px and fade in
Doors1600 msThe website intro
Film5 s loopSilent, no captions naming dishes
  • Nothing bounces.No spring overshoot, no shake, no spin.
  • One move at a time.A photo pushes in, or the words rise. Not both.
  • Transform and opacity only.Smooth at 60 frames a second, on any phone.
  • Reduced motion means still.Everything rests in its final place, nothing stays hidden.

Six textures

The restaurant’s own surfaces, photographed flat. They give a piece depth under its ground, never a message of their own.

  • Pattern under a wash.Lay the texture first, then a ground wash over it: night at 70–75% on dark, ivory at 80–85% on light. The pattern should read as a quiet weave, not as a picture.
  • Text always on a wash.Headings, labels and body sit on the wash. Never place the pattern directly behind body text, and never put a dark texture under dark type.
  • One texture per piece.Dark textures for night and cobalt grounds, light ones for porcelain and ivory. Keep the 16:9 crop and never stretch it.

Menu system

Six chapters, stacked like a tingkat. Names as the family says them, one plain line each.

  1. 01Small plates & kuihBaba Delight platter · Kuih Pie Tee · Jiu Hu Char · Otak-Otak · Egg Belanda
  2. 02Curries & mainsCurry Kapitan · Nyonya Curry Chicken · Nyonya Beef Rendang · Gulai Tumis · Assam Prawns · Sambal Udang · Sambal Petai Sotong · Ikan Goreng Kunyit · Chye Por Omelette
  3. 03Soup & vegetablesKiam Chai Ark · Terung Sambal · Kerabu Pucuk Paku · Sambal Kangkung
  4. 04RiceNasi Ulam · Kerabu Bee Hoon · Nyonya Fried Rice
  5. 05DessertsBee Koh Moy · Sago Gula Melaka · Pengat
  6. 06DrinksRose Selasih · Nutmeg Juice · Bandung
  • Dish namesBodoni 500, 22–28 pt, as the family writes them. Malay or Hokkien name second, in italic.
  • DescriptionsJost 400, 10–11 pt. What the dish is, in one line. Ingredients, not adjectives.
  • PricesJost 500 tabular, right-aligned, RM, no decimals. Prices are set by the restaurant on the day.
  • Every menu ends with“Dishes change with the market; ask the team for today’s menu and prices.”
